CEO Buys

Not real-time. CEO buys appear here within about 2 minutes of being filed, but the law lets CEOs file up to 2 business days after the trade. Politician trades appear 2 to 7 weeks after. Why?
  • The law allows a delay. CEOs have 2 business days to file a Form 4. Members of Congress have up to 45 days, and the President's reports often come later still.
  • This site checks on a timer. With the live feed on (Vercel hosting), it checks the SEC every 60 seconds. Without it (GitHub Pages only), it checks every 10 minutes in US market hours, hourly otherwise, and GitHub sometimes runs late.
  • Nobody legally sees these earlier. The SEC posts a filing to everyone at once. Paid terminals are faster at reading it, not earlier at getting it.
  • Prices are end of day. Charts and "since buy" use daily closing prices, not live quotes.
  • Some filings can't be read by machine. Scanned PDFs (common for the President and some House members) need a person to check them.

What this page is

This page tracks when executives buy their own company's stock. They know their business better than anyone, so when they put their own money in, it can mean they see better days ahead.

Repeat and cluster buys

The same company seeing more than one insider buy, or the same CEO coming back for more.

Bought the dip

Insiders buying right after a sharp price drop. Only trades where we know how far the stock had fallen.

When they buy

One bar per day, by the date the trade happened. Weekends are dimmed because markets are closed.

Bar height is scaled so small buys stay visible. Filings follow within two business days, so a buy shows up on this page a day or two after it happened.

Read this before you copy a trade

  • A CEO buying is a signal, not a promise. Intel's CEO bought at $95 in August and at $64.73 in November. Both buys were losing money when the second was filed. FourWeekMBA
  • The signal is weaker at big companies. Research finds insider buying predicts returns more strongly at smaller companies. Interactive Brokers summary
  • Unusual buys matter more than habits. A Harvard and NBER study found trades that break an insider's usual pattern carried the predictive power, while routine, yearly-habit trades earned about nothing. Cohen, Malloy and Pomorski (NBER)
  • A buy can come with strings. HubSpot's Brian Halligan has to pay the company back about $450K in short-swing profits because of earlier sales. The Cerbat Gem

How to read this page

Form 4
The form insiders must file with the SEC within 2 business days of buying or selling their company's stock.
Open-market buy
The insider paid market price with their own money. Free shares from pay packages, option exercises and tax sales are left out.
Fallen from high
How far below a recent peak the stock was when they bought. The card says which peak was used.
Possible reasons
Our guesses from the numbers. Each one shows how we know and where the numbers came from. The insider did not give these reasons.
~ before a price
Worked out as dollar value divided by shares, because the source did not give the exact price.

Read this before you copy a trade

  • Amounts are ranges. "$1,001 - $15,000" could be $1,100 or $15,000.
  • Many trades are made by a spouse or a fund manager, not the politician personally.
  • By the time a trade is public, the price has usually moved. A 45-day-old trade is old news.
